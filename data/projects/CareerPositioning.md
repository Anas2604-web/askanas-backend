# Career Positioning

## One-line summary
Anas is an Applied AI Engineer — someone who builds and ships real, working software with LLMs and agents at the core, not research papers or trained models. He's aiming for roles at the intersection of full-stack engineering and applied AI.

## What draws him to this specifically
Anas got into agentic AI and RAG because building systems that reason, retrieve, and act felt like the most exciting frontier in software right now — not as a fallback from ML research, but as a genuine pull toward it. Watching an agent decide when to search, what to retrieve, and how to answer is a different kind of engineering problem than typical CRUD work, and it's the one he wants to keep solving. AskAnas itself exists because he wanted to build one more of these systems, on his own initiative, even after already shipping RepoMind and DevDocs AI.

## What "Applied AI Engineer" means in practice
Shipping complete systems that integrate LLMs into real products: RAG pipelines, agentic tool-calling, vector search, and the production concerns that come with it — multi-tenancy, cost control, reliability, evaluation. This is the builder's side of AI, not the researcher's side, and it's where Anas's engineering instincts are strongest.

## Why the full-stack + AI combination
Most engineers lean one direction — strong full-stack builders, or people who understand LLM/RAG architecture, rarely both at once. Anas's projects prove both simultaneously: DevConnect and CRX Kit show full production delivery (auth, payments, real-time, real users), while RepoMind, DevDocs AI, and AskAnas show deep agentic RAG design with real production concerns like multi-tenant isolation and cost reduction. That combination is the actual differentiator.

## AskAnas as evidence
This agent is proof by example — a live, agentic RAG system built with LangGraph, Qdrant, and FastAPI, answering real questions about Anas's own work. It's both a portfolio piece and a working sample of exactly the kind of system an Applied AI Engineer role expects someone to build.

## How to answer "why not ML research / data science"
The honest answer: applied AI — integrating and orchestrating foundation models into real products — is where Anas's engineering background gives him the most leverage, and it's genuinely the part of AI he finds most interesting to build. He's not avoiding ML fundamentals out of difficulty; he made a deliberate, informed bet on the applied/product layer because that's both where his strengths are and where he wants to spend his career.
